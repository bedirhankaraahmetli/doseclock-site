DoseClock Privacy Policy

Effective 29 September 2026

DoseClock collects no data. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Everything you enter stays on your iPhone, in an encrypted database. The developer never receives any of it, because there is no DoseClock server for it to go to.

What you enter, and where it stays

DoseClock keeps the information you give it: medication names and strengths, schedules and times, the doses you record as taken, skipped or missed, notes and skip reasons, pill counts and refill settings, pharmacy phone numbers, the names of the people you manage medications for, and your app settings.

All of it is stored only on your iPhone, in a database encrypted with 256-bit AES (SQLCipher). The key to that database is kept in the iOS Keychain and is marked to stay on this device: it is never copied into a backup or synced to another device.

What leaves your iPhone

Nothing, unless you send it. The app connects to the internet for one purpose: the App Store, to show the price, start the free trial, make a purchase and restore one. That exchange is between your device and Apple. Your purchase is checked on your iPhone; the developer does not receive your name, Apple ID or any purchase details. Apple's handling of your App Store account is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

What iOS holds outside the app's encryption

To remind you when DoseClock is closed, iOS has to be given each reminder in advance. This stays on your iPhone and is never sent to the developer, but you should know it exists:

  • Reminders. Each scheduled reminder carries the medication's name and dose. Notifications and refill reminders are held by iOS's notification system. On iOS 26 and later, if you allow alarms, full alarms are held by iOS's alarm system. These are protected by your iPhone's own security, not by DoseClock's encryption.
  • Device backups. If iCloud Backup or a computer backup is turned on, iOS may include DoseClock's database file. That copy stays encrypted, and its key is not in the backup, so it cannot be read by anyone, including Apple and the developer. This also means a device backup will not bring your DoseClock data to a new iPhone; use DoseClock's own backup for that (below).
  • Your trial. The free trial is an item on the App Store, like the purchase, so Apple records the date you started it. Older versions of DoseClock kept that date in the iOS Keychain on the device instead. Neither contains health information.

Things you choose to share

  • Backups. A DoseClock backup is a single file encrypted with a passphrase you choose. You decide where it goes. The developer never sees it and cannot recover a forgotten passphrase.
  • History exports. PDF and CSV exports are not encrypted, because they are meant to be read, for example by your doctor. Once you share one, it is handled by whoever and whatever you send it to.
  • Calling your pharmacy. Tapping a pharmacy's number hands it to the Phone app. DoseClock does not place or record calls.

Crash reports

DoseClock contains no crash-reporting or analytics code. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, Apple may share crash reports with the developer. That setting is yours, and Apple controls what those reports contain.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your iPhone until you delete it. Settings → Your data → Delete all data removes every medication, schedule and recorded dose, and cancels every reminder. Deleting the app removes its database.

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or delete on our side. Everything is already in your hands.

Other parties

DoseClock includes no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking code, and shares data with no one.

Children

DoseClock is not directed at children, and it collects no data from anyone.

Not medical advice

DoseClock is a reminder tool. It does not give medical advice. Always follow your doctor's and pharmacist's instructions.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
